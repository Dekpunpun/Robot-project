# The Vesper Manifest — Art & Theme Brief

**One-line pitch:** A hardboiled military-noir detective mystery — a sergeant's
wife goes missing four days before someone starts stealing demolition charges
off an army base, and you have one night to find out which of four soldiers
is lying.

## Setting

Harrow's Reach, a fog-and-lamplight port city built around Fort Callow, an
active military installation. Mid-20th-century feel — service caps, chevrons,
patrol jackets, a 2.5-ton truck in the motor pool — but not tied to a specific
real war or decade. Think "the noir genre's idea of a military town" rather
than a history lesson.

## Palette — "Warm Amber Noir"

Cold dark-blue night as the base (near-black, never pure black) punched
through by warm lamplight — browns, golds, amber. The detective's coat is the
only consistently saturated color in most scenes. UI is amber phosphor on warm
charcoal, like a police-station CRT terminal. Nothing neon, nothing pastel.
Rain reads as cold blue-white streaks against the warm lamp pools; fog is a
desaturated grey-blue haze.

## Mood

Weary, procedural, quietly moral. Not pulpy or campy — the violence is
implied, not shown. Endings are bittersweet at best (the case closes but
nothing is undone) and can be hollow even when you're right. Every character
is protecting something understandable, even the guilty one.

## The Detective (player)

Long coat, fedora, plain — deliberately unremarkable so the player can project
onto them. No name given.

## The Four Suspects

How each should read visually, at a glance:

- **Staff Sergeant Elias Thorne** — broad, weathered, olive field uniform,
  sergeant's chevrons. The culprit — should look ordinary, not villainous.
- **Corporal Wyatt Doss** — lean, tan working fatigues, wrench on his belt.
  The vault NCO — practical, a little nervous.
- **Colonel Margaret Ashworth** — upright, army service dress, brass buttons,
  peaked officer's cap. Command presence — the only one who outranks the
  detective's expectations.
- **Lance Corporal Sam Bricker** — hunched, mechanic's coveralls,
  grease-stained. The one whose innocence isn't really the point — he's
  protecting someone else's secret, not his own guilt.

## Locations

Each should feel distinct at a glance:

- **Fort Callow Vault** — concrete, steel racking, padlocked gun lockers,
  stenciled ammo crates. Two conspicuously empty rack slots.
- **Fort Callow Motor Pool** — same concrete, a truck in the end bay, oil
  drums.
- **Fort Callow Command Office** — carpet, a wall map with one red pin on it.
- **Third Precinct** (home base) — wood floor, a corkboard with red string
  between photographs.
- **14 Milner Street** (Thorne's house) — ordinary home, a kitchen that's been
  disturbed, a garage hiding a burner phone.
- **Salt Row, Dock 4** (fishing cabin) — weathered wood, a bunk, a lit stove,
  bollards and rope out front on the dock.
- **Forensics Lab** — pale tile, black-topped bench with glassware, an
  evidence cabinet, a microscope.

## Weather / Atmosphere

Rotates between clear, rain, and fog — never sunny, never daytime. Dust motes
drift through every lamp's light pool. Lamps flicker faintly, never perfectly
steady.

## What to Avoid

Bright saturated colors, clean/modern architecture, anything that reads as
fantasy or sci-fi, gore, cartoonish exaggeration. This is restrained and
procedural, not stylized-cute or grimdark-extreme.
